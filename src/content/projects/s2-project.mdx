## The brief

Every organization has a pile of documentation that people can't find their way through: policies, runbooks, specifications, handbooks. A chatbot over that pile is one of the most common LLM features a team is asked to build, and one of the easiest to build badly. It will answer every question fluently. Some answers will come from the right page, some from an outdated one, and some from nowhere at all.

You will build a question-answering system over a real public document set. The deliverable is not a chatbot that sounds right. It is a system that answers from its sources, proves it with citations your code has checked, says "I don't know" when the sources don't settle the question, and produces measurements that show how often each of those things happens.

This system has a second life. In Studio 3 you will build an evaluation harness around it, find its failure modes systematically, and improve it. So it must log everything that harness will need. Treat the trace log as a product requirement, not a debugging aid.

## Choose your corpus

Pick one of these, or propose another public corpus with a license you have checked. Licenses were checked on 2026-11-11; confirm them again for what you download, and record the license in your README.

| Corpus | Where | License | Why it's interesting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Python Enhancement Proposals (PEPs) | [github.com/python/peps](https://github.com/python/peps) | Each PEP states its terms in its Copyright section; most are public domain or CC0-1.0-Universal | Hundreds of documents, reStructuredText, explicit status fields (Final, Superseded, Rejected), so outdated-source traps come built in |
| Kubernetes documentation | [github.com/kubernetes/website](https://github.com/kubernetes/website) | CC BY 4.0 (attribution required) | Large Markdown corpus with versioned content, heavy jargon, and many near-duplicate pages; good for hybrid search |
| GOV.UK Service Manual | [gov.uk/service-manual](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual) | Crown copyright under the Open Government Licence v3.0 (some content is exempt; check the GOV.UK terms) | Plain-language guidance for non-specialists; questions are phrased very differently from the text |
| The Rust Programming Language (book) | [github.com/rust-lang/book](https://github.com/rust-lang/book) | MIT or Apache 2.0 | A long, structured book in Markdown with code; tests whether your chunking respects code blocks and sections |

Download from the source repositories rather than scraping rendered pages, keep the attribution the license requires, and don't commit large corpora to your repo; commit a download script instead.

## Suggested architecture

Week 4's lesson "Designing the Q&A system" walks through each stage and the log record. Lab 04 (cited answers) gives you the generation and verification step; the week 1 to 3 labs give you embeddings, chunking, hybrid search, and reranking.

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the eval set on day one">
Before any code, write 30 questions a real user of your corpus would ask, and label each with the document, the section, and the exact quote that answers it. Include questions the corpus can't answer and questions where an older document disagrees with a newer one. Then build retrieval and measure recall at k before you generate a single answer. Every later decision (chunk size, k, reranker, model) becomes a number instead of an impression.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Pitfalls that sink this project">
- **Tuning the prompt when retrieval is broken.** If the gold passage never reached the model, no prompt fixes the answer. Check recall at k first.
- **Chunk ids that change.** Sequence-number ids shift whenever you add a document or change chunk size, and your eval labels and old logs stop matching. Derive ids from the document, section, and a hash of the text.
- **Verifying against the wrong text.** Check each quote against the passage it cites, not against all passages joined together.
- **Treating abstention as failure.** A system that never says "I don't know" is answering questions it can't answer. Measure abstention in both directions.
- **Ignoring document status.** Superseded PEPs, old Kubernetes versions, and archived pages quote perfectly and are wrong. Carry status and dates from ingestion to the prompt.
- **Logging only successes.** A trace log without the abstentions, errors, and verifier failures measures nothing.
</Callout>

## Constraints

- Use the official Anthropic SDK for generation (or another provider's official SDK if you prefer, but justify it). Embedding and reranking providers are your choice; justify them with retrieval measurements. Keep keys in environment variables.
- A full evaluation run over your eval set should cost under $1 with your chosen configuration, not counting one-time embedding of the corpus. Log it and prove it.
- The offline test suite must run without an API key, using a fake client and fixture responses.
- Respect the corpus license: keep required attribution in your README, and don't redistribute content in ways the license doesn't allow.

## Write-up template

Put this in `WRITEUP.md` in your project repo and fill it in. Aim for two pages.

```markdown
# Q&A over [corpus]: write-up

## Problem
Who would use this, what they ask, and what "good" looks like. (2 to 3 sentences)

## Corpus
Source, size (documents, tokens), license and attribution, and what makes it hard.

## Approach
- Architecture (diagram or bullets)
- Ingestion: parsing, chunking, id scheme, metadata
- Retrieval: retrievers, fusion, reranker, k (and why)
- Generation: model(s), prompt version, citation method, abstention rule

How you wrote it, composition (answerable, multi-passage, unanswerable, traps), how gold evidence is labeled.

## What Studio 3 should look at first
```
