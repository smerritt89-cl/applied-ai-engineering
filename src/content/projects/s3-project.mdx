## The brief

Your Studio 2 project answers questions over a document set and cites its sources. You probably believe it works reasonably well. This project replaces that belief with a measurement, and then uses the measurement to make the system better.

The deliverable is not a score. It is an **instrument**: a harness you trust enough that, when it says a change helped, you would bet on it, and when it says nothing changed, you would believe that too. Then you use it at least twice: two improvements, each stated as a hypothesis before the run, compared on the same questions with confidence intervals, and confirmed on data you didn't tune against.

You will also explain the results to someone who doesn't build software. As a PM leader you will spend more time presenting eval results than computing them, and an eval that only engineers can read changes fewer decisions than it should.

Build it in its own repository (`rag-eval-harness`) that imports or calls your Studio 2 system, or as an `evals/` folder inside your Studio 2 repository. Either is fine: choose the one that makes the CI workflow simplest, and say which in your write-up.

## What you get

Everything here builds on work you have done:

- **Lab 04 (Studio 3, week 4)** gives you retrieval metrics, claim-level faithfulness with a structured-output judge, judge agreement, a bootstrap, and a report generator. Reuse it.
- **Week 2's labs** give you judge calibration and the corrected-rate estimator; **week 3's** give you paired comparisons and regression testing.
- **Lesson 10** covers review sampling and closing the loop; **lesson 11** covers which RAG metrics measure what; **lesson 12** walks through the harness design, the gate rule, the log, and the stakeholder summary.

What you need to supply: your Studio 2 system, and a person to act as domain expert. If your corpus is something you know well, that can be you, but say so, and have at least 20 labels double-checked by someone else.

<Callout type="tip" title="Measure the baseline before you change anything">
Your first logged run is the unmodified Studio 2 system on dev and held-out. Every improvement is a comparison against it (or against the most recent adopted baseline). Resist the urge to fix things you notice while building the dataset: write them down as hypotheses instead. They are your improvement candidates.
</Callout>

## Pitfalls

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Overfitting to the eval set">
After many rounds of tuning against the same dev questions, the dev score reflects those questions more than the system. The sign is a dev score that keeps climbing while held-out barely moves. Keep the held-out split sealed, log every variant you try, and report the held-out result as the headline.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Judge drift">
If you edit the judge's prompt, switch its model, or the mix of questions shifts, its scores move even when the system doesn't. Pin the judge version in config and in every log row. Any judge change means: recalibrate against expert labels, rescore the baseline, and only then compare.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="Faithful but wrong, and the always-answer system">
A system can be perfectly faithful to the wrong passage. Keep a correctness check against reference answers on at least part of the set. And without should-abstain questions, a system that always answers looks better than one that knows its limits; report abstention both ways.
</Callout>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="A gate that reacts to noise">
A rule like "block if the mean drops at all" blocks on random variation and soon gets ignored. Use paired intervals and a tolerance, block only on clear drops or failed regression cases, and route uncertain results to a person.
</Callout>

## Constraints

- Use the official SDKs for any model calls, with keys in environment variables locally and repository secrets in CI.
- The fast CI suite must run in under five minutes and cost less than 25 cents. A full judged run over dev and held-out should cost under $5 at current prices; log it and prove it.
- The offline test suite (code graders, statistics, gate rule, report formatting) must run without an API key, using recorded outputs or a fake client.

Put this in `WRITEUP.md` and fill it in. Aim for two pages plus the appendix.

```markdown
# RAG eval harness: write-up

## Stakeholder summary (read this first)
One paragraph for a non-engineer: the decision you recommend, what changed for users in plain language
("about N in 100 answers..."), how sure you are (a range, in words), cost, the biggest remaining risk,

What the Studio 2 system does, who uses it, and what "good" means for them. (2 to 3 sentences)

- Dataset: version, size, composition by source, how labels were made and by whom, dev / held-out split
- Graders: one line per metric, code or judge, and how each was validated
- Judge calibration: model and prompt version, labeled set size, TPR and TNR with intervals, raw vs corrected rates
- Detectable difference: the smallest change your set can distinguish, and how you worked it out
- Where it lives: repo or folder, and why you chose that
```
